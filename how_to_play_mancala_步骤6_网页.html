What Is Mancala?

Mancala (pronounced mahn-KAH-lah) is a family of two-player strategy board games that share a common mechanic: picking up stones from a pit and sowing them one by one into other pits around the board.

A 6,000-Year Legacy

Mancala is widely considered one of the oldest known board games in human history:

  • 6000–7000 BCE: Mancala-like boards were carved into stone floors in ancient Jordan.
  • Ancient Egypt: Boards resembling Mancala have been found carved into temple roofs along the Nile.
  • Africa and the Middle East: The game spread through trade routes, evolving into variants like Oware (West Africa), Bao (East Africa), and Kalah (the version most commonly played in the US).

The name “Mancala” comes from the Arabic word naqala, meaning “to move.” This guide covers the Kalah variant, the most popular version in America.

What You Need to Play Mancala

Before you start playing, you need just two things: a Mancala board and 48 stones or seeds.

The Mancala Board

A standard Mancala board consists of:

  • 12 small pits (also called “houses” or “pockets”) arranged in two rows of six
  • 2 large pits (called Mancala stores)—one on each end of the board

    |  1 |  2 |  3 |  4 |  5 |  6 |
    +----+----+----+----+----+----+
       → Player 1's pits (left to right)

Each player “owns” one row of six pits and the Mancala store to their right.

The Stones

  • You need 48 stones (or seeds, marbles, glass beads, beans—anything small and uniform will work).
  • Most commercial sets include colored glass stones or natural pebble-like seeds.
  • Some premium sets include up to 60+ stones to account for spares.
Tip: You don’t need anything fancy to start. Dried beans, pennies, or even small buttons work perfectly as Mancala stones.

How to Set Up Mancala

Setting up a game of Mancala takes less than one minute. Follow these steps:

  1. Place the board between you and your opponent. The board should be oriented so the long rows of six pits face each player, with one large Mancala store on each end.
  2. Identify your territory. The six small pits closest to you are yours. Your Mancala store is the large pit to your right.
  3. Place 4 stones in each of the 12 small pits. That is 4 stones × 12 pits = 48 stones total. Both Mancala stores should be empty at the start.
  4. Decide who goes first. Use a coin flip, rock-paper-scissors, or let the younger player go first.

That’s it. Your board is set and you are ready to play.

Mancala Rules: Step-by-Step Gameplay

Mancala is played in turns. On your turn, you pick up stones from one of your pits and sow them around the board. Here is exactly how it works.

The Basic Move: Sowing

  1. Choose a pit on your side of the board that contains one or more stones.
  2. Pick up all the stones from that pit. The pit is now empty.
  3. Sow the stones one at a time, dropping one stone into each subsequent pit as you move counterclockwise around the board.
  4. Drop a stone into your own Mancala store as you pass it (this is how you score points).
  5. Skip your opponent’s Mancala store—you never place stones in their store.

Example: If you pick up 4 stones from your leftmost pit, you drop one stone in each of the next four pits going counterclockwise. If one of those stones lands in your Mancala store, great—that’s a point earned.

Capturing Stones

This is where Mancala gets exciting. If your last stone lands in an empty pit on your side of the board, you capture all the stones in the pit directly opposite (on your opponent’s side) plus the stone you just placed. All captured stones go into your Mancala store.

Example: Your last stone lands in your third pit, which was empty. The pit directly across from it (on your opponent’s side) has 5 stones. You capture all 5 of those stones plus your 1 stone, adding 6 stones to your Mancala store. That is a huge swing.

Extra Turns

If your last stone lands in your own Mancala store, you earn an extra turn. You get to go again immediately. Skilled players chain multiple extra turns together in a single sequence, sometimes emptying the entire board in one dramatic combo.

Game End

The game ends when all six pits on one player’s side are empty. When this happens:

  • The other player collects all remaining stones from their side and places them in their own Mancala store.
  • Both players then count the stones in their Mancala stores.
  • The player with the most stones wins.

Since there are exactly 48 stones in play, you need 25 or more stones in your Mancala to win. Exactly 24 stones each is a tie.

Special Rules You Must Know

Three special rules give Mancala its strategic depth:

1. Landing in Your Own Mancala = Extra Turn

Whenever the last stone in your sowing lands in your Mancala store, you take another turn immediately. This is the single most important mechanic in the game. Plan your moves so you frequently earn extra turns, and look for opportunities to chain them together in a single sequence.

2. The Capture Rule

If your last stone lands in an empty pit on your side, you capture all stones from the pit directly opposite on your opponent’s side, plus your sowing stone. Both go into your Mancala store. Key points:

  • You only capture if the opponent’s opposite pit is not empty.
  • Captures can dramatically shift the stone count and are often the difference between winning and losing.

3. The Empty Pit Trap

Be cautious about leaving stones where your opponent could capture them. If you have a vulnerable pit and your opponent is setting up a sowing that will land across from it, consider moving those stones first. Conversely, create empty pits on your side to set up your own future captures.

5 Winning Strategies for Beginners

Now that you know the rules, here are five battle-tested strategies that will elevate your game from “just playing” to “playing to win.”

Strategy 1: Start with the Rightmost Pit (or the Third Pit)

The most common opening moves in Mancala are:

  • Pit 6 (rightmost pit): If you pick up the 4 stones from your rightmost pit, the last stone lands in your Mancala store, giving you an extra turn. This is the simplest and most popular opening move.
  • Pit 3 (middle-left pit): Sowing from this pit places your last stone on your opponent’s side, which sets up potential future captures and does not waste a pit that could give you an extra turn later.
Pro tip: Experienced players often alternate between these openings depending on their opponent’s style.

Strategy 2: Maximize Extra Turns

Extra turns are the most powerful advantage in Mancala. To earn them consistently:

  • Count before you sow. Always check if any pit has exactly the right number of stones to reach your Mancala.
  • Set up chains. Use your first extra turn to pick up stones from another pit that also reaches your store, creating a chain reaction.
  • Save extra-turn pits. If a pit could give you an extra turn but you don’t need it right now, hold it for a better moment.

Strategy 3: Control the Board with Counting

Mancala is ultimately a counting game. The best players constantly track how many stones are in each pit, which of their opponent’s pits are empty (making captures dangerous), and which of their own pits could become empty for a future capture. Spend a few seconds counting before every move.

Strategy 4: Build “Stone Heaps” for Captures

Having pits with many stones (8+) is strategically valuable. When you sow from a loaded pit, stones travel far around the board, often landing in empty pits on your side for a capture. Large heaps also give you flexibility and make your opponent cautious about leaving stones across from you. Avoid sowing from your biggest pit too early.

Strategy 5: Defend Your Store and Deny Your Opponent

Defense in Mancala means:

  • Don’t leave stones vulnerable. If your opponent could capture across from one of your pits, move those stones first.
  • Block extra turns. Disrupt your opponent’s chains by changing the stone count in key pits.
  • Empty pits strategically. An empty pit on your side means your opponent cannot capture across from it.

People Also Ask

Is Mancala easy to learn?

Yes, Mancala is one of the easiest strategy board games to learn. The basic rules can be explained in under 5 minutes: pick up stones, sow them one by one, and collect the most in your store. Children as young as six can grasp the mechanics quickly, while the game still offers enough strategic depth to keep adults engaged.

What age can kids play Mancala?

Children can start playing Mancala as young as 5 to 6 years old. At this age, kids understand counting, taking turns, and the basic sowing mechanic. Mancala is widely used in elementary classrooms across the US to develop counting skills and strategic thinking. Most commercial sets are recommended for ages 6+.

How long does a Mancala game take?

A typical game lasts 5 to 15 minutes. Quick games between experienced players can finish in as little as 3 to 5 minutes. This short pace makes Mancala perfect for filling time, and it is easy to play multiple rounds in one sitting.

Is Mancala a game of skill or luck?

Mancala is overwhelmingly a game of skill. There is no dice, cards, or random element. Every outcome is determined by the players’ decisions, meaning a skilled player will consistently beat a beginner. However, the game has thousands of possible states and is far from “solved,” so there is always room to improve.
