The Three Routes to Sublimation Printing Explained

Before diving into specific models, you need to understand the fundamental difference between these three approaches:

  • EcoTank Conversion — You buy a standard Epson EcoTank inkjet printer and replace the factory ink bottles with third-party sublimation ink. It is the cheapest entry point, but it comes with real trade-offs.
  • Epson SureColor F-Series — Epson’s factory-built sublimation printers ship with genuine UltraChrome DS ink and full warranty support. You pay more upfront, but everything works out of the box.
  • Sawgrass — A dedicated sublimation brand that designs printers from the ground up for dye-sublimation. Sawgrass machines include proprietary ink management software and color profiles tuned specifically for heat-transfer work.

Each route produces vibrant, permanent, full-color prints on polyester fabrics and polymer-coated substrates. The differences lie in cost, convenience, reliability, and long-term scalability.

Route 1: Epson EcoTank Conversion — The Budget Hacker’s Choice

How It Works

Models like the Epson EcoTank ET-2800 and the wide-format ET-8550 use refillable ink tanks instead of cartridges. You drain the factory ink, flush the print heads, fill with sublimation ink from suppliers like Hiipoo or Cosmos Ink, and install a color profile for accurate transfers.

Pros

  • Lowest entry cost. An ET-2800 runs roughly $180–$200 retail, and a set of quality sublimation ink costs $20–$35. Total startup under $240.
  • Cheap per-page printing. Third-party sublimation ink averages $0.03–$0.05 per 8.5×11″ page, far cheaper than branded ink systems.
  • Wide model selection. The ET-8550 prints up to 13×19″, giving you large-format capability at a fraction of a dedicated wide-format sublimation printer’s price.

Cons

  • Voided warranty. Epson explicitly states that using non-Epson ink voids the manufacturer warranty. If a print head clogs permanently, you are on your own.
  • Color profiling headaches. Out of the box, converted EcoTanks often produce shifted colors — reds lean orange, blues go purple. You will need to create or download ICC profiles, and results vary by ink brand, paper, and substrate.
  • Maintenance burden. Sublimation ink dries faster than standard inkjet ink. If you do not print at least every 2–3 days, you risk clogged nozzles that require multiple cleaning cycles to clear.
  • No official support. Epson customer service will not troubleshoot sublimation-related issues on a converted EcoTank.

Best For

Hobbyists and side-hustle sellers who print 10–50 items per month, are comfortable with DIY troubleshooting, and want to minimize upfront investment.

Route 2: Epson SureColor F-Series — The Official Epson Sublimation Path

Does Epson Make a Sublimation Printer?

Yes. The Epson SureColor F170 (letter-size) and F570 (24-inch wide-format) are purpose-built dye-sublimation printers using Epson’s UltraChrome DS ink technology. Unlike the EcoTank hack, these machines carry a full Epson warranty, official technical support, and factory-calibrated color profiles.

Epson SureColor F170

  • Price: Approximately $399 retail
  • Max print width: 8.5 inches
  • Ink system: UltraChrome DS (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black) in 140ml bottles
  • Ink cost per page: Roughly $0.07–$0.10 per letter-size page
  • Warranty: 1-year limited warranty with Epson support

The F170 is widely recommended by creators like Jennifer Maker as the best budget-friendly entry point for anyone who wants a true sublimation printer with manufacturer backing. It connects via USB or Wi-Fi and works with Epson’s Edge Print software for color management.

Epson SureColor F570

  • Price: Approximately $2,495 retail
  • Max print width: 24 inches
  • Ink system: UltraChrome DS in 500ml pouches
  • Ink cost per page: Roughly $0.04–$0.06 per letter-size equivalent (cheaper due to larger ink pouches)
  • Warranty: 1-year limited warranty with advance exchange

The F570 targets production environments — think custom apparel shops, sign makers, and print-on-demand businesses. The 24-inch width handles full-size garment panels, banners, and large photo prints that no letter-size machine can touch.

Pros

  • Warranty and support. Epson stands behind these printers. If something breaks, you have a path to resolution.
  • Accurate color out of the box. Factory ICC profiles deliver reliable results on standard sublimation paper and substrates without manual calibration.
  • Reliable ink delivery. UltraChrome DS ink is engineered for sublimation, reducing clogs and maintenance compared to third-party inks in converted printers.
  • Software ecosystem. Epson Edge Print (included with F170) and professional RIP options (for F570) give you precise control over color, density, and print settings.

Cons

  • Higher ink cost. Genuine UltraChrome DS ink costs 2–3x more per page than third-party sublimation ink used in conversions.
  • F170 is small format. The 8.5-inch max width limits you to standard paper sizes. No 13×19, no 24-inch rolls.
  • F570 is a major investment. At nearly $2,500, it is not realistic for hobbyists or casual sellers.

Best For

Small business owners and serious crafters who value reliability and support over the absolute lowest ink cost. The F170 fits sellers doing 50–200 items per month; the F570 suits production shops doing 200+ items.

Route 3: Sawgrass — The Purpose-Built Sublimation Specialist

Sawgrass SG500 and SG1000 Overview

Sawgrass is the only company that designs printers exclusively for sublimation printing. The SG500 (letter/A4 size) and SG1000 (up to 11×17″ with optional bypass tray) use Sawgrass’s proprietary SubliJet-UHD ink, which is formulated specifically for the printer’s piezo-electric print heads.

Pros

  • Best-in-class color accuracy. Sawgrass printers ship with ICC profiles tuned to SubliJet-UHD ink and common substrates. Colors are consistently accurate without manual profiling.
  • CreativeStudio software. Sawgrass includes a design tool with built-in templates, product mockups, and color management — valuable for beginners who do not have Photoshop or Illustrator.
  • Two-year warranty. Longer than Epson’s one-year coverage, and Sawgrass support understands sublimation-specific issues.
  • Lowest maintenance. SubliJet-UHD ink is engineered to resist clogging. Users report fewer nozzle issues than with both converted EcoTanks and SureColor F-Series printers.

Cons

  • Highest ink cost. SubliJet-UHD cartridges are the most expensive per page of any option in this comparison. At $0.15–$0.20 per letter-size page, your costs add up quickly at production volume.

Best For

Beginners and intermediate sellers who prioritize color accuracy, ease of use, and peace of mind over per-page cost. The SG500 is ideal for Etsy sellers and craft fair vendors doing 20–100 items per month.

Which Route Is Right for You? A Decision Framework

Rather than declaring a single “best” sublimation printer, the honest answer depends on where you are in your sublimation journey:

Choose EcoTank Conversion If:

  • Your monthly budget for supplies is under $50
  • You are comfortable watching tutorials and troubleshooting independently
  • You print fewer than 50 items per month
  • You want to test sublimation before committing to a business-grade setup
  • You value the lowest possible per-page cost above all else

Choose SureColor F-Series If:

  • You need manufacturer warranty and technical support
  • You want accurate colors without manual ICC profiling
  • You are ready to treat sublimation as a revenue-generating business
  • The F570 specifically suits you if you need large-format prints for apparel, signage, or home decor products

Choose Sawgrass If:

  • You are a complete beginner and want the simplest possible setup
  • Color accuracy is your top priority (e.g., brand-specific merchandise)
  • You value bundled design software (CreativeStudio)
  • You print 20–100 items per month and can absorb higher ink costs

People Also Ask: Answers to Your Sublimation Printer Questions

Is it better to buy a sublimation printer or convert an Epson?

It depends on your budget and tolerance for risk. Converting an Epson EcoTank like the ET-2800 costs roughly $225 total (printer plus sublimation ink) and gives you the cheapest per-page cost in sublimation printing — around $0.03–$0.05 per page. The trade-off is a voided warranty, manual color profiling, and higher maintenance. Buying a purpose-built sublimation printer like the Epson SureColor F170 ($399) or Sawgrass SG500 ($575) gives you warranty coverage, factory color accuracy, and official support — but at 2–5x the per-page ink cost. For hobbyists printing fewer than 50 items per month, conversion makes financial sense. For anyone running a business where downtime costs money, a factory-built machine is the safer investment.

What is the difference between Sawgrass and Epson sublimation printers?

The core difference is ecosystem philosophy. Sawgrass uses a closed system — proprietary SubliJet-UHD cartridges with firmware locks that prevent third-party ink. This ensures consistent color accuracy and minimal maintenance, but locks you into the highest ink cost in the market ($0.12–$0.20 per page). Epson’s SureColor F-Series uses UltraChrome DS ink in refillable bottles, which costs less per page ($0.04–$0.10) but still requires genuine Epson ink for warranty compliance. Sawgrass offers a 2-year warranty versus Epson’s 1-year coverage, and includes CreativeStudio design software. Epson provides broader RIP software compatibility for professional workflows. In short: Sawgrass prioritizes simplicity and color accuracy; Epson prioritizes flexibility and lower operating costs.

Does Epson make a sublimation printer?

Yes, Epson makes two purpose-built sublimation printers under the SureColor F-Series brand. The SureColor F170 is a desktop-sized model ($399) that prints up to 8.5 inches wide using UltraChrome DS ink in 140ml bottles. The SureColor F570 is a large-format model ($2,495) that prints up to 24 inches wide using 500ml ink pouches. Both carry full Epson warranties and official technical support. Epson also manufactures the EcoTank line of refillable inkjet printers (ET-2800, ET-8550, etc.), which are not designed for sublimation but can be converted by replacing the standard ink with third-party sublimation ink — though this voids the warranty.

Final Recommendation: The Best Sublimation Printer for Your Situation

If we had to pick one sublimation printer for most people starting a sublimation business in 2026, the Epson SureColor F170 hits the sweet spot. It offers genuine Epson quality, full warranty coverage, reliable color output, and a reasonable $399 price tag. The per-page ink cost sits in the middle of the pack, and the setup takes about 10 minutes.

For absolute beginners on a tight budget, a converted EcoTank paired with a quality heat press gets you printing for under $250 — just be prepared to invest time in learning color management and performing regular maintenance.

For production shops ready to scale, the SureColor F570 delivers the lowest per-page cost among factory-built options and handles large-format work that no other printer in this comparison can touch.

And for sellers who want the simplest, most hands-off experience with the best out-of-box color accuracy, the Sawgrass SG500 remains a solid choice — as long as you can stomach the premium ink pricing.

Whichever route you choose, pair your printer with a reliable heat press and quality sublimation paper, and you will be producing vibrant, professional-grade merchandise from day one.
