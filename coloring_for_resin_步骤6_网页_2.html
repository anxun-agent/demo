Method 1: Liquid Resin Dye — Clean, Transparent Color

Liquid resin dye is the most popular coloring for resin among beginners and professionals alike. These concentrated dyes are formulated specifically for epoxy resin, mixing in smoothly without clumping or streaking.

10 Colors/Set Translucent Concentrated Resin Colorant

Featured Product: 10 Colors/Set Translucent Concentrated Resin Colorant — US $5.65 for 10 colors

How It Works

Liquid dyes are added drop by drop to your mixed resin. Start with 1–2 drops per ounce of mixed resin and build up from there. The color disperses evenly with gentle stirring — no special tools required.

Results

  • Transparent finish that lets light pass through
  • Smooth, even color with no visible particles
  • Colors layer beautifully for gradient effects
  • Will not settle at the bottom of your mold

Mixing Ratio & Tips

  • Ratio: 1–3 drops per oz of mixed resin for transparent color; 5–8 drops for deeper saturation
  • Stir gently for 60–90 seconds to avoid introducing air bubbles
  • Colors appear darker once cured — always test a small batch first
  • Mix colors together to create custom shades (e.g., 2 drops blue + 1 drop green = teal)

Pros & Cons

Pros Cons
Easiest to mix Limited color range per set
Even, streak-free color Can look washed out in thin pours
Won't affect curing Some dyes fade under UV over time
Affordable per project
Best for: Beginners, transparent jewelry pieces, coasters, bookmarks, and any project where you want clean, glass-like color.

Method 2: Mica Powder — Shimmer & Pearlescent Effects

Mica powder is a mineral-based pigment that gives resin a beautiful shimmer and pearlescent glow. It's one of the most versatile coloring options for resin, especially when you want sparkle and depth.

6 Pcs/Set Epoxy Resin Dye Mica Powder Pearlescent

Budget Pick: 10g 28 Colors Mica Powder Natural Pearl Powder — US $2.03 for a single 10g jar

How It Works

Mica powder is a fine, dry powder that you add directly to mixed resin. Use a small spatula or toothpick to scoop the powder in, then stir thoroughly. The particles catch and reflect light, creating that signature shimmer effect.

Results

  • Pearlescent, shimmer finish that shifts with viewing angle
  • Semi-opaque to opaque coverage depending on amount used
  • Creates beautiful "galaxy" and "ocean" effects
  • Visible sparkle particles that add depth and dimension

Mixing Ratio & Tips

  • Ratio: 1/8 to 1/4 teaspoon per oz of mixed resin
  • Mix powder with a tiny amount of isopropyl alcohol first to create a paste — this prevents clumping
  • Stir slowly and thoroughly for at least 2 minutes
  • Powder settles over time in the mold, so pour soon after mixing
  • Layer different colors for stunning gradient effects

Pros & Cons

Pros Cons
Stunning shimmer effect Can clump if not pre-mixed
Huge color variety available Settles at bottom in thin pours
Very affordable per use Creates opaque finish at higher amounts
Lightfast and UV-resistant Fine powder can be messy
Best for: Galaxy art, ocean pour paintings, jewelry with shimmer, keychains, and decorative home pieces.

Method 3: Alcohol Ink — Diffusion & Watercolor Effects

Alcohol ink is the secret weapon behind those stunning "petri dish" resin art pieces with organic, flowing tendrils of color. The alcohol-based formula interacts with resin in unique ways that no other colorant can replicate.

10ml Epoxy Resin Diffusion Pigment Alcohol Ink

Featured Product: 10ml Epoxy Resin Diffusion Pigment Alcohol Ink — US $0.99 per bottle

How It Works

Unlike other colorants that you mix directly into resin, alcohol ink is typically dropped onto the surface of poured resin. The alcohol evaporates and carries the pigment into the resin, creating organic cells, tendrils, and watercolor-like diffusion patterns.

Results

  • Watercolor-like diffusion with organic, flowing patterns
  • Transparent finish with visible depth
  • Creates unique cells and lacing effects
  • Each pour is one-of-a-kind — impossible to exactly replicate

Mixing Ratio & Tips

  • Ratio: 2–4 drops on the surface of each 4 oz of poured resin
  • Pour your clear (or lightly tinted) resin first, then drop ink onto the surface
  • Use a heat gun or torch on low to encourage ink to spread and create cells
  • Do NOT stir — stirring destroys the diffusion effect
  • Combine with white liquid dye underneath for dramatic contrast with colored tendrils on top
  • Work in a well-ventilated area — alcohol fumes are strong

Pros & Cons

Pros Cons
Unique, artistic effects Results are unpredictable
Creates organic patterns Requires more practice
Very affordable Alcohol fumes require ventilation
Beautiful layered depth Not ideal for uniform color
Best for: Petri dish art, abstract ocean art, statement jewelry, and any project where you want organic, one-of-a-kind patterns.

Method 4: Metallic Pigment — Gold, Silver & Luxury Finishes

If you want your resin pieces to look like molten metal, metallic pigments are the way to go. These concentrated pigments contain fine metallic particles that create a reflective, chrome-like finish.

50g Shimmer Metallic Gold Silver Pearlescent Pigment

Featured Product: 50g Shimmer Metallic Gold Silver Pearlescent Pigment — US $2.92

How It Works

Metallic pigments come in powder or paste form and are mixed directly into your resin. The metallic particles suspend throughout the resin, catching light from every angle for a true metallic sheen.

Results

  • True metallic finish — looks like brushed gold, silver, copper, or bronze
  • Opaque coverage that completely conceals the resin beneath
  • Creates dramatic contrast when swirled with clear resin
  • Professional-looking results with minimal effort

Mixing Ratio & Tips

  • Ratio: 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon per oz of mixed resin
  • Add pigment to Part A before mixing with Part B for easier dispersion
  • Stir for a full 2 minutes until no streaks remain
  • For a marble effect, mix metallic pigment into only half your resin and swirl with clear
  • Works especially well with silicone molds that have geometric patterns

Pros & Cons

Pros Cons
Luxurious metallic finish Opaque — no transparency
Easy to mix Limited to metallic color palette
Excellent coverage Can separate in slow-curing resin
Works with any mold type Slightly more expensive per oz
Best for: Luxury jewelry, geode art, metallic coasters, pen blanks, and accent details in larger pieces.

Method 5: Chameleon / Color-Shifting Powder — Iridescent Magic

Chameleon pigments are the showstoppers of the resin coloring world. These specialized powders shift between two or more colors depending on the viewing angle, creating an iridescent, almost holographic effect that changes as the piece moves.

Mirror Chameleons Resin Pigment Mica Powder

Featured Product: Mirror Chameleons Resin Pigment Mica Powder — US $0.99 per color

How It Works

Chameleon powder uses multi-layer optical pigments that refract light differently at different angles. Mix the powder into your resin as you would mica powder — the key difference is the dramatic color shift that appears once the piece is cured.

Results

  • Color-shifting iridescent effect — one color face-on, another at an angle
  • Creates a sense of depth and movement
  • Semi-opaque with a mirror-like sheen
  • Extremely eye-catching for statement pieces

Mixing Ratio & Tips

  • Ratio: 1/8 to 1/4 teaspoon per oz of mixed resin
  • Less is more — too much powder will muddy the color-shift effect
  • Pre-mix with a drop of isopropyl alcohol to prevent clumping
  • Works best in thicker pours (1/4 inch or deeper) where light can interact with the particles
  • Black or dark background enhances the color-shift effect dramatically
  • Use a toothpick to create swirls that emphasize the directional color change

Pros & Cons

Pros Cons
Stunning color-shift effect More expensive than standard mica
Unique, premium look Can look muddy if over-mixed
Lightfast and durable Effect less visible in thin layers
Great conversation starter Limited color options available
Best for: Statement jewelry, custom phone cases, premium keychains, and any project where you want a high-end iridescent finish.

Method 6: DIY Alternatives — What Actually Works?

If you're wondering how to dye resin without dye, you're not alone. Many crafters experiment with household items. Here's an honest breakdown of what works and what doesn't.

Acrylic Paint

  • Result: Opaque, chalky color with a matte finish
  • Verdict: It works in a pinch, but acrylic paint contains water, which can interfere with the curing process. Use no more than 6% of your total resin volume to avoid sticky, uncured resin.
  • Tip: Choose heavy-body acrylics over student-grade for better pigment concentration.

Eye Shadow / Loose Cosmetic Pigment

  • Result: Similar to mica powder — shimmery and pearlescent
  • Verdict: Surprisingly effective! Many cosmetic-grade eye shadows contain mica and work well in resin. Crush powder shadows finely before mixing.
  • Tip: Test for colorfastness — some cosmetics fade under UV exposure.

Food Coloring

  • Result: Weak, translucent tint
  • Verdict: Not recommended. Food coloring is water-based and can prevent proper curing. Colors also fade rapidly.

Powdered Spices (Turmeric, Paprika)

  • Result: Warm, earthy tones with organic texture
  • Verdict: Partially works for a rustic look, but colors will fade within weeks and particles create a grainy texture.

Can You Use Regular Dye in Resin?

No — regular fabric dye or Rit dye should not be used in resin. These dyes are water-based and will not properly bond with epoxy resin. The water content can interfere with the chemical curing reaction, leaving you with soft, tacky resin that never fully hardens. Always use colorants specifically formulated for resin, or use alcohol-based inks as an alternative.

People Also Ask

How to dye resin without dye?

If you don't have resin-specific dye, you can use several alternatives. Alcohol ink works well for diffusion effects — drop a few drops onto poured resin and let it spread naturally. Mica powder or even crushed eye shadow can add shimmer. Acrylic paint works in small amounts (under 6% of total volume), though it may slightly affect curing. Avoid water-based colorants like food coloring, as the water prevents proper resin curing. For the best results on a budget, a $0.99 bottle of concentrated liquid colorant goes a long way.

Can you use regular dye in resin?

No. Regular dye — such as fabric dye (Rit), hair dye, or clothing dye — is water-based and should never be used in epoxy resin. The water content interferes with the chemical reaction between Parts A and B, which can result in soft, sticky, or incompletely cured resin. Additionally, these dyes are not designed to bond with resin polymers, so the color will separate or bleed. Always choose colorants labeled as resin-safe, alcohol-based, or oil-based.

How do I color resin transparent?

For transparent resin color, use liquid resin dye — it's specifically designed to tint resin while maintaining transparency. Add 1–2 drops per ounce of mixed resin and stir gently. Avoid mica powder and metallic pigments, as these create opaque or semi-opaque results. For a watercolor-transparent look, try dropping a small amount of alcohol ink onto the resin surface without stirring.

Can I mix different coloring methods together?

Yes! Some of the most stunning resin art combines multiple methods. A popular combination is liquid dye for a transparent base color, with mica powder or metallic pigment swirled in for shimmer accents. You can also use alcohol ink on top of resin tinted with liquid dye for dramatic contrast. Just make sure all colorants are resin-compatible (not water-based).
